Shopify Apps and Integrations: Choosing the Right Approach

A new feature doesn't automatically mean another app. The right path depends on data, scale, performance, and who will manage the solution long-term.

AuthorJakub HrnčířUpdated13 August 2026Reading time5 minutes

The Shopify ecosystem offers thousands of ready-made apps, custom theme customizations, and the ability to create a custom app. The goal is not to choose the most technically interesting variant, but the simplest solution that reliably covers the task and does not unnecessarily complicate the operation.

Four basic options

01
Apps from the Shopify App Store

A ready-made app is suitable for a common use case that its developer maintains across many stores.

02
Editing a theme

Liquid and JavaScript can add storefront presentation and interaction without another paid tool.

03
Custom app

A custom app suits specific logic, data processing or functionality that ready-made apps do not cover.

04
External integration

An external integration connects Shopify with shipping, ERP/PIM, email, payment, analytics or other systems.

When to choose a ready-made app

An out-of-the-box app is often the fastest solution for reviews, loyalty programs, emailing, product feeds, or common sales functions. But it is not enough to read the description in the Shopify App Store before installing. Check pricing for your volume, data allowance, theme compatibility, uninstall method and support availability.

Each app adds another vendor and potentially more scripts. Five well-chosen apps can be better than one large, tailor-made solution. Twenty overlapping apps already often create unnecessary costs and unclear responsibilities.

When is theme editing enough?

If the function mainly concerns the display of the product, content, sections or interaction in the customer-facing storefront, customizing the Shopify theme may be more suitable. The advantage is the precise connection to the design and a smaller number of external dependencies. The disadvantage may be the need for maintenance during a major redesign or theme change.

When does a custom app make sense?

A custom app is suitable when a store needs its own data logic, Shopify admin, automation or specific connections. Development makes sense especially when ready-made apps require complex compromises, their combination is more expensive or the process is part of the company's competitive advantage.

Since 1 January 2026, new custom apps are registered and managed in the Shopify Dev Dashboard. A developer can create an app there or start the project with Shopify CLI and connect it to the Dev Dashboard. Earlier legacy custom apps can still be managed directly in Shopify admin. Development should therefore use the current permission model, versioning and deployment workflow.

How to think about integration

For an integration, the system name matters less than the data flow. Which system is the source of truth? What is synchronised, how often, and what should happen when an error occurs? Shipping, ERP/PIM, email marketing, payment services and analytics each have very different requirements.

  • Determine the source of truth for products, prices, inventory, and customers
  • Describe the direction and frequency of synchronization
  • Resolve duplicates, outages and repeat requests
  • Limit permissions to only necessary data
  • Prepare test environment, logging and support responsibilities

Decision rule

Start with the smallest solution that meets the requirement without major compromise. If the finished app covers a standard process and has quality support, in-house development may not bring value. If the need is part of a unique process or connects key data, a short-term saving on technical design may become more expensive later.

A good Shopify integration is not what the user sees. It's the one you don't have to think about during normal operation.

Frequently asked questions

When are apps from the Shopify App Store enough?

A finished app makes sense if it solves a common process, supports the necessary markets, and its operating costs and limitations are acceptable. It's a good idea to check support, permissions and theme impact before installing.

What is the difference between an app and an integration?

The app adds functionality to Shopify. The integration connects Shopify with another system and handles data flow, such as products, warehouse, customers or orders. One solution can fulfill both roles.

Can apps slow down an online store?

Some apps load scripts or elements in the customer-facing storefront, others hardly affect its performance. Therefore, it is better to measure the specific impact than to automatically remove all apps.
