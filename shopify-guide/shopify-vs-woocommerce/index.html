Shopify vs WooCommerce

Shopify offers the operational simplicity of a hosted platform. WooCommerce provides control through an open-source WordPress stack. The central difference is who owns the technical responsibility.

AuthorJakub HrnčířReviewed13 Aug 2026Reading time7 minutes

Shopify and WooCommerce are more than two different dashboards. Shopify is hosted: infrastructure, platform security and core deployment are handled by Shopify. WooCommerce is a free open-source WordPress plugin where the merchant chooses hosting and owns the complete technical stack. That distinction shapes cost, freedom and daily maintenance.

Quick answer

Shopify is usually the better fit when you want to reduce server and platform-core maintenance. WooCommerce offers greater control over code and hosting, but requires a team or supplier to own updates, security, performance and extension compatibility.

What operating the store actually costs

WooCommerce core is free, but the store is not cost-free. You need a domain, hosting, SSL, backups and often paid themes or extensions. More complex projects add staging, monitoring, development and compatibility work. The benefit is the freedom to choose suppliers and technical components.

Shopify has a predictable platform subscription that includes hosting and SSL. Apps, themes, development and payment fees are additional. For both platforms, compare total cost of ownership rather than the core licence or entry plan.

Maintenance and security

WooCommerce documentation recommends current WordPress, PHP and database versions and warns that unsupported versions can create security risk. The merchant or supplier coordinates hosting, updates, plugins, backups and testing. For larger stores, Woo recommends staging and/or version control.

Shopify removes server and platform-core maintenance, but it does not remove all maintenance. Apps, custom theme code, integrations, analytics and storefront changes still need review.

Customisation and development

WooCommerce gives teams broad control over WordPress, PHP, the database and hosting. It fits projects closely connected to a large content site or teams that deliberately want ownership of the server layer. That freedom creates more options and more components requiring specialist care.

Shopify limits the server layer but supports extensive storefront work with Liquid, JavaScript, custom sections, apps and APIs. Brands often benefit from developers focusing on customer experience and integrations instead of basic server operations.

SEO and performance

Both platforms can be fast and crawlable. WooCommerce offers deep control, while outcomes depend on hosting, themes, plugins and configuration. Shopify automates part of the technical foundation, but excessive scripts, apps or an unoptimised theme can still reduce performance. SEO alone is not enough reason to choose either platform.

Practical verdict

  • Shopify: when you want hosted commerce, lower infrastructure overhead and development focused on the storefront and business workflows.
  • WooCommerce: when you need ownership of the full stack, already have WordPress expertise and accept operational responsibility.
  • Audit first: when you already use one platform. Migration only makes sense if it solves a defined constraint.
Open source is not automatically cheaper, and hosted commerce is not automatically inflexible. The decision turns on required control and team capacity.

Frequently asked questions

Is WooCommerce really free?

WooCommerce core is free and open source. Running a store still requires WordPress hosting and may involve paid themes, extensions, development, backups and maintenance.

Is Shopify easier to maintain?

Shopify manages hosting and the platform core, so merchants do not maintain the server, WordPress or plugins. Themes, apps, integrations and content still require ongoing management.

Which platform is better for SEO?

Both can perform well. Content, structure, internal links, speed, technical implementation and ongoing management matter more than the platform logo.

Technical decision

Considering a move from WooCommerce?

First identify whether the issue is the platform, hosting, plugins or implementation. Only then can the migration scope be estimated responsibly.
