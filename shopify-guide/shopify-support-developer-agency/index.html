Shopify Support vs Developer vs Agency

Shopify Support, the theme author, the app developer, and the person managing the custom code handle different parts of the store. A quick fix starts with correctly assigning responsibility.

AuthorJakub HrnčířUpdated29 July 2026Reading time6 minutes

When a shopping cart, app or part of a theme stops working in an online store, it is not always clear who should resolve it. Shopify provides the platform and support for its standard features, but cannot assume responsibility for any custom code or third-party app.

What to expect from Shopify Support

The extent of support depends on the theme and request type. For free Shopify themes, support may review some predefined customizations. For a paid theme, support usually moves to its author. Shopify states that it does not undertake modifications or diagnostics of significantly changed themes, third-party apps, and third-party custom code.

This is not to say that Shopify Support is inadequate. It means that platform support and custom development are two different services.

First, determine the source of the problem

The same error can occur in a theme, app, embedded script, product settings, or an external system. Before editing, make a safe copy of the theme and find out when the problem started, which pages it affects, and if it is related to the last change.

  • Write down the exact address and steps leading to the error
  • Specify the device, browser and expected result
  • Check for the last theme publication, app installation, or setting change
  • Verify the issue on a clean preview without interfering with the live store
  • Determine who owns the affected code or integration
  • After the fix, test related purchase scenarios

What ongoing technical maintenance includes

Maintenance is not just about responding to errors. It includes an overview of your own code, secure deployment, app control, change testing and documentation of decisions. Shopify allows themes to be duplicated, and versioning via GitHub can also be used for custom development.

A regular check should look for unused apps, old embedded code, slowdowns, broken links, and changes to external services. Frequency depends on the pace of campaigns and development; a small stable store does not need the same regime as an online store with weekly deployments.

A clear brief speeds up the work

Instead of "the site is down", send the address, steps, expected behavior, screenshot, priority and deadline. Add information about recent changes and approaches that the solver will need. Do not email passwords; use secure user or peer access.

Technical support works best when it has a clear owner, a secure deployment method, and a definition of what is done.

Frequently asked questions

Who should fix an error in a Shopify theme?

It depends on the origin of the problem. For a free theme, Shopify Support can help with selected modifications, for a paid theme, its author, and for custom code, a developer who can safely diagnose the change.

How do I back up a Shopify theme before editing?

The theme can be duplicated or downloaded before changing. For long-term managed custom code, versioning, for example via GitHub, also makes sense.

Is a one-time repair or regular maintenance better?

A one-time job is enough for a clearly defined requirement. Regular capacity is useful if changes are recurring, linked to campaigns, or require continuous review of apps and custom code.
